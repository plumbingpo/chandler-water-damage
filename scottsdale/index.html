The First Hour After Water Damage in Scottsdale

Typical Water Damage Problems in Scottsdale

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones Scottsdale property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Scottsdale, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. We remove any standing water in Scottsdale homes, dry the walls and slab, and explain what likely caused it.

How Drying Works on a Scottsdale Water Damage Job

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. Our job on every Scottsdale property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. Most homes in Scottsdale reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.
